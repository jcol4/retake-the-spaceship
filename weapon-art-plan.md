# Weapon Art Plan — v1: the five-weapon family

*Written against the Assault Rifle reference supplied 2026-07-27. Companion to
`character-art-plan.md` and to the gameplay docs under
`docs/design/factions/contractors/weapons/`. Models are generated by Blender scripts under
`tools/`; everything below is a specification for those scripts, not hand-modelled geometry.*

> ## ⚠️ WRITTEN FOR THE 3D PIPELINE, WHICH IS GONE
>
> This document specifies **Blender-generated weapon meshes for a rigged 3D character**. The
> game is now presented isometrically, with 2D sprite characters — see

## 1. What we are building

Five weapons ([roster](docs/design/factions/contractors/weapons/README.md)): Assault Rifle,
Battle Rifle, SMG, Shotgun, LMG. Every soldier can carry any of them, chosen on the pre-mission
loadout screen.

The hard constraint: **all five share one animation rig and one animation set.** No weapon gets
its own clips. That is not a limitation to work around — it is the thing that makes a five-weapon
loadout system affordable at all, and it is cheap to honour if the interface is defined up front.

Reload animations are explicitly deferred. When they arrive they will be per-weapon (a pump-action
shotgun cannot share a mag-swap clip), but nothing in this document should assume they exist.

## 2. What the reference establishes

Two colourways of the same weapon: a dark gunmetal three-quarter view, and a tan/grey side profile
with parts exploded out. Layout is conventional — stock, grip, trigger, magwell, receiver,
handguard, muzzle, front to back — not bullpup.

### 2.1 The signature is negative space

The two things that make this weapon readable at a glance are both **holes**:

1. **The skeletonized stock** — a large triangular void through the buttstock, leaving a thin
   frame around it. Not a solid block with a notch; a genuine opening you see background through.
2. **The enclosed trigger guard** — a big, angular, fully-closed loop, noticeably larger than a
   real-steel trigger guard, integrated into a solid frame plate rather than being a thin wire bow.

At the game's camera distance a weapon is 40–60 px. Surface detail is invisible at that size; a
void in the outline is not. **These two holes are the highest-value geometry in the entire weapon**
and should be built before any greeble.

This has a direct technical consequence — see [Section 7.1](#71-negative-space-needs-a-new-primitive).

### 2.2 Two-tone, and the tone follows function

The reference is not decoratively two-coloured. Colour tracks what a part *is*:

| Role | Reference (dark) | Reference (tan) | Parts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frame / furniture** | Dark gunmetal | Khaki-tan | Stock, pistol grip, receiver spine, trigger-guard plate |
| **Hardware** | Light grey | Light grey | Magazine, vented handguard, side panels, sights, grip panel |
| **Working surfaces** | Near-black | Near-black | Muzzle device, butt pad, textured grip/stock inserts |

Three values, consistently applied. This is a rule the other four weapons inherit verbatim.

### 2.3 Repeated slots and segmentation

- The forward handguard is a **louvered shroud** — five or six horizontal slots cut through a
  grey panel.
- The receiver sides are **segmented into blocks** with visible gaps between them, rather than
  being one smooth extrusion.
- The magazine carries **horizontal witness ribs** along its curve.
- Near the muzzle, a band of **diagonal serrations**.

All four are the same idea: a repeated element along an axis. That is one primitive, reused
(see [Section 7.2](#72-the-repeat-primitive)).

### 2.4 Other reference details

- Magazine is **curved and strongly forward-raked** — roughly 25–30° off vertical, not a straight
  box hanging down.
- **Full-length top rail** with a stepped, uneven upper line: a rear sight block near the middle,
  a front sight forward, gaps between.
- Stock has a distinct **dark rubber butt pad** capping the rear.
- Pistol grip carries a **dark textured panel** on its front face, distinct from the grip body.
- Heavy edge wear throughout — deferred, see [Section 8](#8-deferred).

### 2.5 Red accents — the family tie-in *(added from the 2026-07-27 batch)*

Three of the four later references carry small **red** details: a selector switch on the SMG, a
ring and an underside button on the Battle Rifle, a dot on the Shotgun. They are tiny — a few
pixels each at game distance — and none of them is load-bearing for silhouette.

They matter anyway, because `character-art-plan.md` §1.1 makes **red glowing lenses** the
character's single strongest identity cue. A matching red mark on every weapon ties gun to
operator without spending any silhouette budget, and it costs one existing material (`lens`).

**Family rule:** every weapon carries exactly one small red element, placed near the receiver where
it sits above the firing hand. Not emissive at full lens strength — the lenses must stay the
brightest red on screen — so use `lens` colour at reduced emission, or a non-emissive red.

This supersedes the khaki tie-in idea from the first draft; see [Section 3](#3-palette--the-references-dark-colourway-is-wrong-for-us).

## 3. Palette — the reference's dark colourway is wrong for us

This is the one place we should deliberately diverge from the reference.

The soldier is near-black (`suit` = 0.075, `vest` = 0.105 in `gen_soldier.py`'s `MATERIALS`). At
the game camera the weapon overlaps the torso in most poses. **A near-black weapon against a
near-black torso has no silhouette at all** — the two merge into one dark mass and the weapon
stops reading, which defeats the entire point of five visually distinct weapons.

So: keep the reference's *structure* but invert which value dominates.

| Weapon part | Material | Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magazine, vented handguard, receiver side panels, sights | `pad` (0.165 grey) | **Light** | The primary read. These are the parts that must separate from the torso. |
| Stock frame, receiver spine, pistol grip, trigger-guard plate | `dark` (0.038) | Dark | Recedes. Carries the voids, which read as outline regardless of value. |
| Muzzle device, butt pad, grip insert | `dark` | Dark | Working surfaces, as reference. |
| One small red element per weapon | `lens`, reduced emission | Accent | The tie-in to the character's red lenses. See §2.5. Must stay dimmer than the lenses themselves. |
| Optional: one khaki element | `pouch` | Mid | Sparingly, and only where it doesn't compete with the chest rig. The LMG's muzzle device is the one place the reference clearly calls for it. |

Note this lands close to the reference's *dark* colourway (grey mag + grey handguard against a
dark body), just with the grey deliberately load-bearing rather than incidental. The tan colourway
is the wrong direction for us for a different reason: khaki is already the character's
identity-defining accent per `character-art-plan.md` §1.1, and a fully-tan weapon would compete
with the chest rig for that role.

**Constraint:** `pad` grey is currently used for knee pads. If weapon-grey and knee-pad-grey read
as the same material at distance that is fine — both are "kit," and one shared material keeps the
atlas small. No new material is needed for the weapon family.

## 4. The rig interface — the contract all five obey

Coordinate convention (Blender, Z-up), unchanged from the existing rifle: **origin at the trigger
/ firing-hand web, barrel down +Y, sights up +Z, X lateral.** Part half-extents are
`(half_width_X, half_height_Z)`.

| Anchor | Value | Varies per weapon? |
|---|---|---|
| `GRIP` | `(0, 0, 0)` — the origin | **Never.** Identical origin and basis means the solved mount transform in `scenes/character_base.tscn` is reused verbatim by all five. |
| `SUPPORT` | **400 mm from `GRIP`** — measured, see below | **Never.** This is the one that actually breaks animations. |
| `MUZZLE` | barrel tip | **Yes** — becomes `WeaponData` data, not a hardcoded scene node. |

### 4.1 Measured, 2026-07-27 — and it needed a pipeline fix first

`tools/measure_support.py` samples the left hand's position in right-hand space across every
weapon-holding clip. The first run found the anchor was **not achievable as authored**:

| | as downloaded | after the lock |
|---|---|---|
| hand separation across clips | 306–467 mm | **394–400 mm** |
| worst disagreement between two clips | 321.9 mm | **7.5 mm** |
| handguard length needed to cover it | 400 mm | **98 mm** |

Mixamo does not constrain hands to a prop. Each rifle clip was authored independently, so hand
separation — which two hands on one rigid weapon physically cannot change — drifted from 317 mm in
`idle` to 466 mm in `aim_hold`. No handguard covers a 150 mm swing; a weapon built for the aim pose
would show the support hand visibly off the weapon at idle and run.

Fixed in `tools/build_anims.py` (`lock_support_hand`): the left arm is re-solved so the wrist sits
at a fixed offset from the right hand, using the mocap elbow as the IK pole so the animator's arm
character survives and only the wrist moves. **This is a one-time correction to the animation, not
per-weapon data** — the one-rig constraint is intact.

```
SUPPORT_OFFSET   = (-0.097290, 0.368303, 0.120797)   # metres, right-hand space
SUPPORT_ROTATION = ( 0.665569, -0.327842, 0.612605, -0.272492)
```

Target derived from `shoot_recoil` + `stand_to_crouch` + `crouch_to_stand`, which agree to 49 mm
and 7.6° and sit at the median ~400 mm — so locking there asks the smallest correction of the
466 mm and 317 mm outliers. Verified visually with `tools/render_pose.py --proxy`, which draws the
grip axis both hands should be holding: both hands land on it in every clip, with no broken elbows.

**Family rule:** every weapon places a graspable handguard volume of at least **110 mm** length
centred on `SUPPORT` (the measured 98 mm envelope plus margin), and an enclosed trigger guard at
the origin. Everything else — receiver bulk, stock, magazine, optic, bipod, barrel length — is free.

### 4.2 Consequence: Sections 5 and 6 need a layout revision

The first draft assumed `SUPPORT` at `y ≈ 0.255`, inherited from the old bespoke rig's handguard.
**The measured value is ~400 mm from the grip** — 145 mm further forward.

Every longitudinal layout in Sections 5 and 6 was written against the old assumption and is now
too short ahead of the grip. The Assault Rifle is the clearest case: its handguard spans
`y = 0.185 … 0.345` and its muzzle sits at `0.470`, which would put the support hand past the end
of the handguard and only 70 mm behind the muzzle.

Two ways to absorb it, and this is a real design choice rather than an arithmetic fix:

- **Move the grip back** relative to the receiver, keeping overall lengths. Cheapest, and it suits
  the Shotgun's bullpup layout naturally, but it pushes the other four toward a bullpup-ish look
  they are not supposed to have.
- **Grow the weapons forward.** Keeps the conventional layouts, but adds ~145 mm to every length —
  the AR becomes ~925 mm, which is a battle rifle, and the whole roster's length separation
  (Section 6.5) re-scales with it.

**Resolved 2026-07-28 — see 4.3.** The second option won, and it was settled by measurement rather
than by picking.

### 4.3 Settled by the RifleLow import *(2026-07-28)*

`art_src/RIfleLow.fbx` — an AR-pattern carbine with a full-length handguard — was brought in as the
Assault Rifle via `tools/import_rifle.py`. Scaling it so trigger-to-muzzle matches the blockout's
tuned `0.62 m` gives **s = 0.615**, and its own features then land on the blockout's independently
derived numbers:

| feature | RifleLow @ 0.615 | `gen_rifle.py` blockout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pistol grip | −0.085 … −0.014 | −0.072 … −0.008 |
| magazine | +0.073 … +0.172 | +0.036 … +0.082 |
| stock butt | −0.285 | −0.255 |

Two separately derived figures agreeing is why the scale is trustworthy; a bounding box alone would
have said nothing.

What this settles:

- **Overall length is 905 mm** as imported, against the 925 mm that "grow forward" predicted above —
  the estimate was right to within 20 mm. **Compacted to 745 mm on 2026-07-28** (see 4.4), and the
  roster's length separation (Section 6.5) re-scales off *that*.
- **`SUPPORT` at 400 mm lands mid-handguard**, 220 mm short of the muzzle — comfortably inside the
  furniture at both ends. The 110 mm minimum handguard rule from 4.1 is met with room to spare.
- The handguard no longer has to *absorb variance*. `gen_rifle.py` sized its 0.18–0.54 m handguard to
  contain a hand gap that swung 0.320–0.468 m; `tools/build_anims.py` now pins that gap at ~400 mm,
  so the only surviving requirement is that 400 mm be *on* the handguard.

Sections 5 and 6 still need their coordinate tables rewritten, but the design question is closed:
**conventional layouts, grown forward.**

### 4.4 Compaction — and why it is not a scale *(2026-07-28)*

At 905 mm the rifle read oversized against a 1.78 m character. The obvious fix, a uniform scale to
~84%, is **wrong**: it shrinks the pistol grip (27.1 mm radius) and magazine along with everything
else, and those are the parts sized to a human hand. The hand does not scale with them, so a scaled
rifle reads as a toy and actively fights the finger wrap.

Length has to come out of the parts a hand never touches. `tools/import_rifle.py` `SPLICES` removes
two bands of Y and closes the gaps, keeping the trigger at the origin:

| | removed | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| handguard `+0.250 … +0.370` | 120 mm | muzzle 620 → **500 mm** |
| stock tube `−0.250 … −0.210` | 40 mm | butt 285 → **245 mm** |
| **overall** | 160 mm | 905 → **745 mm** |

Both bands sit inside a measured **vertex-free** run, so the two cut faces are identical and closing
them leaves no seam and no reshaping. Vertex count is unchanged at 897, and the pistol grip's
centroid, axis, length and radius are bit-identical before and after.

`splice()` refuses a band containing vertices rather than flattening them — which caught a first
attempt at the stock, where `−0.200 … −0.160` straddled a vertex plane at `−0.1965`. The usable clear
run there is `−0.2574 … −0.1965` (60.9 mm). **Re-measure both bands if the source model is replaced.**

The support hand, pinned at 380 mm by the animation, does not move — so compaction slides it *forward*
relative to the mesh, from 240 mm short of the muzzle to **120 mm**. Still on handguard, but that is
now the binding constraint on any further shortening, and `import_rifle.py` warns below 50 mm.

## 5. Assault Rifle — full specification

The baseline. Every other weapon is described as a deviation from this one.

Overall length **780 mm** (`y = −0.310 … 0.470`). Reads as a compact carbine.

### 5.1 Longitudinal layout

| Section | `y` range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Butt pad | −0.310 … −0.290 | `dark`. Slightly taller than the stock frame so it reads as a separate cap. |
| Skeletonized stock | −0.290 … −0.105 | **The triangular void.** See 5.2. |
| Receiver | −0.105 … 0.185 | Segmented side panels, `pad` grey over a `dark` spine. |
| Magwell + magazine | 0.055 … 0.100 (well) | Mag rakes forward, see 5.3. |
| Vented handguard | 0.185 … 0.345 | **Contains `SUPPORT`.** Louvered, `pad` grey. |
| Barrel | 0.185 … 0.430 | Round, 8–10 sided, `dark`, mostly hidden inside the shroud. |
| Muzzle device | 0.430 … 0.470 | Stepped compensator block, `dark`. Serration band behind it. |
| Top rail | −0.060 … 0.330 at `z = +0.048` | Stepped upper line, see 5.5. |

`MUZZLE` anchor: `(0, 0.470, 0.014)` — 15 mm further out than the current rifle's 0.455, and
`z = 0.014` is retained since bore height is unchanged.

### 5.2 The stock void

Built as a **frame of three struts around an opening**, not a solid block:

- Upper strut: `y = −0.290 … −0.105` at `z = +0.030`, thin (half-extents ~`(0.014, 0.012)`)
- Lower strut: `y = −0.270 … −0.115` at `z = −0.045`, angled up toward the front so the void is a
  wedge rather than a rectangle
- Rear post: joins the two at the butt pad
- The open front of the wedge tapers into the receiver

Target void size: roughly 130 mm × 55 mm. It needs to be large enough to survive at 50 px —
err toward too big rather than too subtle. This is the single most identifiable feature on the
weapon and is worth 40+ tris on its own.

### 5.3 Magazine

- Enters the magwell at `y ≈ 0.075`, raked **28° forward** of vertical.
- Curved: build as **three segments** with progressively increasing rake (roughly 22°, 28°, 36°)
  rather than one straight box, so the banana curve reads in profile.
- Extends down to `z ≈ −0.235`. Cross-section ~`(0.014, 0.032)` — narrow across, deep fore-aft.
- Four horizontal witness ribs, `dark`, inset from the `pad` grey body.

### 5.4 Grip and trigger guard

- **Pistol grip:** from `y ≈ −0.010` down to `z ≈ −0.145`, raked back 15°. `dark` body with a
  small `dark`-but-textured front panel (at this camera distance the texture distinction is
  material-only, not geometric).
- **Trigger guard:** an enclosed loop, `y = −0.020 … 0.060`, `z = 0 … −0.058`. Built as an arc
  sweep (see 7.1). Interior opening at least 60 mm × 40 mm so the hole is visible in silhouette.
  The reference oversizes this deliberately; we should too.

### 5.5 Top rail and sights

The top line should be **stepped, not flat** — this is what reads as "detailed" at distance far
more than any surface work:

- Base rail: `y = −0.060 … 0.330` at `z = +0.048`, thin.
- Rear sight block: `y ≈ 0.010 … 0.045`, rising to `z ≈ +0.075`.
- Front sight: `y ≈ 0.295 … 0.315`, rising to `z ≈ +0.072`.
- Rail teeth: repeated elements along the base rail — see 7.2. Ten to twelve, small.

### 5.6 Vented handguard

The louvered panel from the reference: **five slots** cut as gaps between six grey blocks running
`y = 0.185 … 0.345`, each block ~22 mm long with ~6 mm gaps. Built as repeats, with the `dark`
barrel visible through the gaps behind — which is what sells them as slots rather than as grooves.

## 6. The other four

*Rewritten 2026-07-27 against per-weapon references. Same vocabulary, same palette, same anchors.
Where a reference contradicts the first draft's spec, the reference wins and the change is noted.*

### 6.1 SMG — *tiny receiver, big front slab*

Reference: suppressed PDW, khaki body with dark grip and internals, a red selector at the rear.

Overall length **580 mm** (`y = −0.250 … 0.330`) — comfortably the shortest in the roster.

**The read is mass distribution, not length.** Almost no receiver, then a large squared slab
hanging off the front. Nothing else in the family is front-loaded like this.

| Section | `y` range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Skeletal stock | −0.250 … −0.090 | Thin frame, `dark` angular butt plate. Carries a void, as the AR. |
| Receiver | −0.090 … 0.090 | Minimal. `dark` spine, `pad` panels. Red selector sits here. |
| Magazine | 0.010 … 0.050 (well) | Short, straight, ~8° forward rake, down to `z ≈ −0.160`. |
| **Suppressor slab** | 0.090 … 0.330 | The identifier. See below. |

**The suppressor slab.** Squared-off, *not* round: half-extents ~`(0.030, 0.038)` — noticeably
fatter than the AR's slim barrel and shroud. Flat-sided, `pad` grey. It carries a **long
rectangular slot cut clean through it** at `y = 0.130 … 0.250`, `z` centred — a third void for the
family, and the largest single opening on any weapon. `MUZZLE` at `(0, 0.330, 0.014)`.

**This solves the `SUPPORT` problem.** The first draft flagged the SMG as the hard case for
reaching the anchor at 520 mm. The suppressor extends the weapon forward past `SUPPORT`, so the
support hand lands on a short foregrip under the slab's rear at `y ≈ 0.255` with room to spare. No
special-casing needed, and no extended handguard hack.

**Dropped from the first draft: the magazine-through-grip idea.** It was wrong for a reason worth
recording — at 40–60 px the firing hand *covers the pistol grip entirely*, so a magazine hidden
behind it contributes nothing to the read. The reference's conventional forward magazine costs
nothing and the slab does all the identifying work.

### 6.2 LMG — *bottom-heavy, belt-fed*

Reference: dark grey, boxy optic, visible ammo belt, folded bipod, khaki muzzle device, broad
solid stock with a wide cheek rest.

Overall length **950 mm** (`y = −0.330 … 0.620`). Widest receiver in the roster — it should look
like it earns the 75% move penalty.

| Section | `y` range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Broad solid stock | −0.330 … −0.140 | **No void.** Wide cheek rest, large flat butt plate. Its identity is mass, not openness. |
| Receiver | −0.140 … 0.200 | Widest in the family, half-width ~0.038 vs the AR's 0.023. |
| **Belt box** | −0.010 … 0.150 | The identifier. See below. |
| Barrel + ribbed shroud | 0.200 … 0.580 | 15+ ribs via `repeat`, reading as a texture band rather than discrete slots. |
| Muzzle device | 0.580 … 0.620 | **`pouch` khaki** — the reference is clearly warm-toned here, and it's the one place khaki earns its place. |

**Belt box, not a drum.** The first draft said drum; the reference is belt-fed. Box sits under the
receiver, `z = 0 … −0.170`, wide (half-width ~0.048 — double the AR's magazine). Its side carries a
row of small round elements standing in for **visible belt links**, done with `repeat`. Squat and
blocky, no curve. This is the primary read.

**Folded bipod** at `y ≈ 0.470`, legs swept back along the barrel. Small, but nothing else in the
roster has geometry below the barrel line forward of the magazine.

**On the optic — recommend shrinking or dropping it.** The reference mounts a squat boxy sight,
but the Battle Rifle's identifier is a tall riser optic (§6.3), and at 50 px "thing on top" may not
resolve into "squat thing" versus "tall thing." The LMG doesn't need it: belt box plus bipod
already separate it from everything. Keeping the top line low buys back a clean silhouette column.
Flagged in [Section 10](#10-open-questions) rather than decided here.

*(The first draft's carry handle is dropped — it was doing the same job as the optic, and the
reference has no such handle.)*

### 6.3 Battle Rifle — *tall optic, longest reach*

Reference: dark blue-grey, large high-mounted sight on a tall riser, slab-sided upper with panel
lines, a prominent **circular** trigger-guard void, skeleton stock with a **rectangular** void,
red ring and underside button.

Overall length **1030 mm** (`y = −0.310 … 0.720`) — the longest in the roster.

**Where the reference and the spec disagree.** The reference weapon is short and thick; the design
brief needs the BR to read as the precision, long-reach pick and to not collide with the AR. So:
**adopt the reference's detail language, extend its proportions.** Barrel runs to `y = 0.680`,
`MUZZLE` at `(0, 0.720, 0.014)`, with the handguard held back so the exposed barrel forward of
`SUPPORT` is what sells the length.

- **Tall riser optic** — round-bodied sight on a visibly tall mount, top of the assembly at
  `z ≈ +0.130`. The tallest top line in the roster by a clear margin. This plus length is the read.
- **Circular trigger guard** — deliberately distinct from the AR's angular loop. Same `arc`
  primitive, full circle, interior diameter ~55 mm.
- **Rectangular stock void** — distinct from the AR's triangular wedge. Slimmer frame, longer
  opening (~150 × 50 mm).
- **Straight box magazine**, short (mag size 3), no curve. `z = 0 … −0.175`.
- **Slab-sided upper** with panel-line segmentation, `pad` grey over a `dark` spine.
- **Red ring** at the receiver above the firing hand — the family accent.

### 6.4 Shotgun — *bullpup, mass behind the grip*

Reference: khaki and black bullpup, large square front housing with a round bore, under-barrel
tube, a prominent row of oval louvre slots along the side, triangular trigger-guard void.

Overall length **770 mm** (`y = −0.290 … 0.480`).

**Bullpup is the big change from the first draft, and it's an upgrade.** Putting the action behind
the trigger inverts the mass distribution — a solid body extending rearward instead of a stock —
and nothing else in the family does that. Combined with the tube it makes this the most distinct
silhouette in the roster.

| Section | `y` range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Receiver body (rearward) | −0.290 … −0.020 | Solid, khaki-and-`pad`. **No stock** — the body *is* the stock. Butt pad caps it. |
| Grip + trigger group | −0.020 … 0.060 | Triangular trigger-guard void. Red dot accent here. |
| Barrel + louvred body | 0.060 … 0.400 | **The oval slot row**, see below. |
| Tube magazine | 0.150 … 0.440 | Under the barrel. Clean, uninterrupted underside — no box protrudes downward anywhere. |
| Front housing | 0.400 … 0.480 | Large squared black block with a round bore opening. Reads heavy and blunt. |

`MUZZLE` at `(0, 0.480, 0.014)`.

**The oval slot row** is the most prominent `repeat` usage in the family — six to seven tall oval
openings along the body side, cut clean through. Larger and rounder than the AR's rectangular
handguard louvres.

**Pump grip** as a ribbed sleeve around the tube at `SUPPORT` (`y ≈ 0.255`) — mechanically honest
and it lands exactly where the anchor needs it.

**Risk to check at step 3:** a bullpup's rear body sits where a stock's butt pad would, right at
the shoulder. It should clear — bullpups are *shorter* for a given barrel, which is the point — but
this is the one weapon in the roster that could clip the chest or shoulder in the aim pose. It
needs a held-pose render before the geometry is considered final.

### 6.5 Silhouette separation check

| | Length | Mass sits | Below barrel | Below receiver | Top line | Stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AR** | 780 | even | — | curved mag | low rail | triangular **void** |
| **BR** | 1030 | even, slim | — | straight mag | **tall riser optic** | rectangular **void** |
| **SMG** | 580 | **front slab** | — | short straight mag | low rail | skeletal **void** |
| **Shotgun** | 770 | **rear (bullpup)** | **tube** | — | low rail | none — body is the stock |
| **LMG** | 950 | **bottom + front** | **bipod** | **wide belt box** | low (see §6.2) | solid, broad |

No two rows match, and every pair differs in at least three columns.

Two pairs are worth watching:

- **AR vs. Shotgun** are within 10 mm of each other in length, so length does no work between
  them. They separate on mass distribution (even vs. rear-loaded), the tube, and the presence or
  absence of a magazine box — three strong columns, so this is fine.
- **BR vs. LMG** are the closest pair overall: both long, both slab-sided. Length (1030 vs. 950)
  is too small a gap to read reliably at 50 px. They rely on bottom mass, width, and the stock
  void. **This is the pair that breaks if the LMG keeps a prominent optic**, which is why §6.2
  recommends dropping it — that keeps the top-line column clean for the BR alone.

## 7. Technical notes for the generator

### 7.1 Negative space needs a new primitive

`build_boxes` produces solid convex boxes between two points. **It cannot make a hole.** Both
signature features of this family — the stock void and the enclosed trigger guard — are holes.

Two options, and we should use both:

- **Frame-of-struts** for the stock void: three or four thin boxes arranged *around* the opening.
  Works today with zero new code. This is how the stock gets built.
- **`arc` primitive** for the trigger guard: sweep a cross-section along a circular arc, N
  segments. A U of three straight boxes would also work but reads angular and cheap on a shape
  that's on screen in every single frame of every animation. Worth the ~30 lines.

`arc` also earns its keep on the LMG carry handle and the drum magazine.

### 7.2 The `repeat` primitive

N copies of a part along an axis, with spacing. Feeds: handguard louvres (AR), rail teeth (all),
mag witness ribs (AR/BR), muzzle serrations (all), pump grip ribs (Shotgun), heat shroud ribs
(LMG). Roughly 15 lines, used everywhere. Highest detail-per-line-of-code in the plan.

### 7.3 Round cross-sections

`build_boxes` already supports an `n`-sided cross-section via its optional `sides` parameter — the
current rifle simply never uses it. Barrels, mag tubes, scope bodies and suppressors should all be
8–12 sided. No new code, immediate fidelity gain.

### 7.4 Bevel

`bevel_mesh()` exists and is applied to the soldier; `build_rifle()` skips it. Applying it to
weapons gives free edge highlights under the rim light. Should be part of the shared build path,
not per-weapon.

### 7.5 Budget

Character target is 6,000–10,000 tris (`character-art-plan.md` §Budget). Weapons should land at
**900–1,400 tris each** post-bevel. At 2–4 units on screen that is negligible.

Materials: **no new materials.** All five weapons draw from `dark`, `pad`, and optionally `pouch`,
all already in `MATERIALS`.

### 7.6 File layout

- `tools/weapon_parts.py` — shared vocabulary: anchors, materials, `build_boxes`, `arc`, `repeat`.
- `tools/gen_weapons.py` — the five spec tables plus `--export` / `--render`.
- `gen_soldier.py` imports the AR spec from `weapon_parts` so pose authoring is unchanged.
- Output: `assets/weapons/{assault_rifle,battle_rifle,smg,shotgun,lmg}.glb`.

### 7.7 Godot side

`WeaponData` gains `model_path: String` and `muzzle_offset: Vector3`. The scene restructures so
the grip transform lives on an intermediate `WeaponMount` node and the weapon mesh becomes an
interchangeable child, with `Muzzle`/`Flashlight` as siblings rather than descendants of the mesh.
Detail in the pipeline discussion; not repeated here.

## 8. Deferred

- **Weathering and edge wear.** The reference is heavily worn; we have no UVs or textures yet.
  This is a texture-atlas pass that should happen for the character and weapons together, after
  camera framing is locked (`character-art-plan.md` §1.3).
- **Reload animations.** Per-weapon, later. Note the Shotgun and LMG will need genuinely different
  clips, and the SMG's grip-fed magazine implies a different hand path than the AR's.
- **Muzzle flash variation.** Currently one flash for all weapons. A shotgun and an SMG plausibly
  want different flash scale/duration, but that is VFX data, not model data.
- **Weapon-specific sounds.** Out of scope for this document.

## 10. Decisions and open questions

### Decided

- **`SUPPORT` is a strict fixed anchor. No per-weapon IK offset.** *(2026-07-27)* Every weapon
  places a graspable volume on the measured point; none of them gets animation-side give. The
  alternative — a per-weapon `hand_ik.L` offset, cheap to implement via
  `tools/add_ik_controls.py` — is rejected for v1 because it reintroduces per-weapon animation
  data, which is exactly the thing the one-rig constraint exists to prevent. Revisit only if a
  weapon proves genuinely unbuildable against the anchor.

  The SMG was the concern here and it has resolved itself: the reference's forward suppressor
  extends past `SUPPORT` with room to spare (§6.1). No weapon in the roster is now known to be
  tight against the anchor.

- **The tie-in accent is red, not khaki.** *(2026-07-27)* See §2.5. Khaki survives only as an
  optional local element, currently just the LMG's muzzle device.

- **The support hand is locked in the animation pipeline.** *(2026-07-27)* `build_anims.py`
  re-solves the left arm onto a fixed grip offset in all nine weapon-holding clips. Necessary
  because the stock Mixamo clips disagreed by 322 mm on where the support hand goes — see
  [Section 4.1](#41-measured-2026-07-27--and-it-needed-a-pipeline-fix-first). Opt out with
  `--no-support-lock`, which restores the un-buildable original drift.
- **The Assault Rifle is an imported model, not a generated one.** *(2026-07-28)* `art_src/RIfleLow.fbx`
  via `tools/import_rifle.py`, at 905 mm. This closes the Section 4.2 layout question with measured
  geometry — see [Section 4.3](#43-settled-by-the-riflelow-import-2026-07-28). The procedural route
  in Section 7 still stands for the remaining four; whether they are authored or sourced is now an
  open choice rather than an assumption.

### Open

- **Is the weapon palette too dark to read against the suit?** `import_rifle.py` reuses the
  blockout's `Metal` (0.115) and `Polymer` (0.080) deliberately, so the model swap changed one
  variable and not two. But those are near-black against a `suit` of 0.075, which is precisely what
  [Section 3](#3-palette--the-references-dark-colourway-is-wrong-for-us) argues against. In the
  aim pose the rifle reads as a silhouette against the lit background and essentially vanishes
  against the body. One-line fix in `import_rifle.py` when the call is made.
- **Does the LMG keep an optic?** §6.2 recommends dropping or shrinking it, against the reference,
  because a tall riser optic is the Battle Rifle's primary identifier and BR vs. LMG is already the
  weakest-separated pair in the roster (§6.5). Decide when both models exist side by side — this is
  a look-at-the-render question, not an argue-it-out question.
- **Should the enclosed trigger guard really be universal?** It is the family signature and it sits
  at the fixed anchor so it costs nothing — but five weapons with an identical trigger group may
  read as lazy at close camera. The references already vary it (AR angular, BR circular, Shotgun
  triangular), which may be enough. Revisit once two weapons exist.
- **Red accent emission level.** It must stay clearly dimmer than the mask lenses or it competes
  with the character's strongest identity cue. The lens material sits at 1.3 emission after
  tonemapper problems at 4.0 (`gen_soldier.py` `MATERIALS`); the weapon accent likely wants
  non-emissive or near-zero. Needs a dark-scene render to judge — `tools/_debug_beam.gd`, since
  `SHOT_CLOSEUP` adds a fill light that washes out exactly this kind of call.
- **Bullpup shoulder clearance on the Shotgun.** §6.4. Should be fine, must be verified with a
  held-pose render before the geometry is final.
- **Scope on the Battle Rifle vs. the Sniper class.** The BR is the Sniper's suggested weapon but
  any class can carry it. A large optic reads as "sniper" regardless of who holds it; acceptable,
  or should it be smaller?
