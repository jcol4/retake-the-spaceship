[notes]
A deck for testing the worm and nothing else. No other alien, no robots, no
rival mercs: anything that MOVES here is a worm.

The one exception is the nest at (4,2), and it is an exception only in the
sense that it is drawn — it has no senses, never acts and never spawns
anything yet, so it cannot interfere with a single thing this deck tests.
It is parked in the spawn room, two tiles north of the light at (4,4), so it
is lit and on screen the moment the deck loads. Move the 'N' anywhere with a
floor if you want to look at it somewhere else.

Run it with:
    godot --path . -- --map=worm_test_deck

Six worms, one per thing worth checking:

(13,2) (18,4) (23,6)  THE DARK HALL. No fixture lights it, so these three can
    only feel you. Walk in and they start crawling; they are the pace test --
    one tile per activation, outrunnable at a stroll. They are also the
    standing-still test: let one close, then kill your flashlight and hold
    still for two turns and it should lose you. Move again and it feels you
    again. (Your own flashlight lights your tile, so it will SEE you at up to
    8 tiles with the beam on -- turn it off or the sense being tested never
    gets a say.)

(9,10)  THE DARK SIDE ROOM. Far from the door, so it is the one to walk past
    at range: outside 4 tiles it never notices you at all.

(16,10) THE CORRIDOR. One tile wide with a door at each end. Nothing to walk
    around, so this is where the bite happens -- it should cost the worm its
    whole activation, one bite, never a step-and-bite.

(24,12) THE LIT ROOM. Standing in the light of the fixture at (25,10), so it
    is the control: normal sight, same as any other alien, and it is the one
    you can actually SEE it clearly against a lit floor.

The platform at (28-29, 10-11), up the stair at (28,12), is for the deck
clause in the sense: standing on it puts you one deck up, and the worm at
(24,12) should not feel you through it however close you stand.


THE TIER GALLERY, through the door at (5,13).

Four alcoves off a lit corridor, one per tier of the worm mass
(docs/design/factions/aliens/design-choices/worm-mass.md), each at the TOP of
its tier so the sculpt is drawn at full scale rather than scaled down:

    (2,16)   5 worms   CLUTCH   1 tile/turn    10 damage    25 HP
    (6,16)   9 worms   KNOT     2 tiles/turn   18 damage    45 HP
    (10,16) 13 worms   SWELL    3 tiles/turn   26 damage    65 HP
    (14,16) 16 worms   TIDE     4 tiles/turn   32 damage    80 HP

Each alcove carries its own overhead light, because the whole point of the
gallery is to LOOK at the four sizes -- this is the one part of the deck that
is not a darkness test. Walk the corridor with the alcove doors in view and
the ladder reads left to right.

The counts come from the `[worms]` section above, not from the grid. A mass is
one WormUnit holding more HP rather than a separate unit type, so there is no
glyph that could say "nine worms here"; the grid marks the tile 'W' like any
other worm and the section attaches the number. A 'W' with no entry is one
worm, which is why the six above still read exactly as they did.

WHY THE GALLERY IS WALLED OFF FROM THE REST OF THE DECK. Masses rally: a worm
that enters combat below terminal walks to the biggest pile in its compartment
instead of at you. Put a Tide in the dark hall and the three pace-test worms
stop being a pace test and become its feeder. One alcove per tier keeps each
ladder rung a thing you can meet on its own.

That isolation is REAL and was checked rather than assumed: MapData.compute_rooms
resolves the four alcoves as rooms 3, 5, 7 and 8, so a mass roused in one of
them cannot call the other three. If that ever stops being true the symptom is
three empty alcoves and one very large pile, and it is a finding about the room
graph rather than about the worm.


THE SIX SINGLES DO NOT STAND ALONE ANY MORE, and the deck notes above were
written before they did anything but crawl at you. The room graph puts

    (13,2) (18,4) (23,6)          in room 0, together
    (9,10) (16,10) (24,12)        in room 1, together

-- the corridor and the lit room are NOT their own compartments the way the
alcoves are. So the moment one of a group notices you, the other two in its
room stop walking at you and start walking at IT, and what the dark hall now
demonstrates is three worms forming a Clutch rather than three worms crawling.

That is the worm behaving correctly, not a broken deck. But it does mean the
pace and standing-still tests above are no longer clean single-unit tests: to
run one properly, meet a worm in an alcove, where it genuinely is alone.

Two tiers are worth meeting in a specific order. The SWELL at (10,16) is the
first rung that one-shots a soldier (26 damage against 19-21 HP) and the first
that moves faster than the merc tier expects. The TIDE at (14,16) is the cap:
it has no attack animation at all, it simply walks through the tile you are
standing on, and four tiles of movement means it can do that to more than one
of you in an activation if you line up.
